# Funny performance-ad strategy

Read this when creating concepts, scripts, hooks, thumbnails, or test variants. The goal is a clear and repeatable commercial idea, not random absurdity.

## Start from one selling equation

For each concept write:

`specific audience + recognizable friction + product mechanism/proof + comic engine + offer/CTA`

The joke must sharpen the audience's recognition of the problem or the product's solution. If removing the product leaves an equally complete sketch, integrate the product earlier or rebuild the payoff.

Select one primary promise and one support point for a short ad. A list of six benefits usually weakens recall and creates claim risk. Translate abstract benefits into visible actions and consequences: less mess becomes one clean pour; faster setup becomes a measured before/after sequence using an honest clock; compactness becomes a verified fit in a real bag or space.

## Four-direction board

Use this optional exploration only when the user requests alternative ideas or has not supplied a chosen direction. If the user gives an idea or script, develop it directly into a complete script for G1; do not force a concept-selection detour. When exploration is requested, create four materially different concepts `C01–C04` before developing the selected direction. Use at least four of the seven modes below unless the user restricts the mode; within a fixed mode, vary premise, character relationship, conflict, product integration, and ending. Four headlines or four openings for the same scene do not meet this requirement.

For every concept show: audience tension, plot mode, hook, character relationship, setup → escalation → turn → ending, product role/evidence, soft-sell or hard-sell treatment, CTA, asset needs, and feasibility for the selected duration. Recommend one using evidence fit, conversational clarity, production complexity, and reuse potential. Use a native Question for selection unless already chosen or delegated, then write the full selected script and proceed to G1.

## Soft-sell, hard-sell, and trending structure

Soft-sell integrates the product naturally into the characters' problem and payoff; avoid interrupting the scene for a feature list. Hard-sell makes the supported benefit, proof, offer, and CTA explicit while retaining motivated dialogue. Neither approach authorizes invented outcomes.

Trending-structure reference is optional and off by default. If enabled, inspect current accessible references and record source/date plus the abstract pacing, reversal, or interaction pattern. Do not claim a format is currently trending without current evidence. Reuse structural principles with original characters and wording, not protected IP, a real person's likeness/voice, an established viral persona, or copied footage. If research is unavailable, label the idea as a general genre pattern.

## Seven selectable plot modes

The reference brief specifies seven modes without naming them. This implementation uses these seven existing comic engines as the selectable modes. Choose one primary mode and optionally a supporting device:

- **Escalating misunderstanding:** each reaction makes the original problem worse until the product makes the situation obvious.
- **Expectation versus reality:** set a familiar category expectation, then reveal a truthful product behavior that flips it.
- **Deadpan absurdity:** stage a ridiculous response with completely serious performance; keep product facts literal.
- **Personified problem:** dust, clutter, heat, cables, or another non-sensitive friction behaves like an antagonist.
- **Failed workarounds:** two or three increasingly silly attempts establish pain before one clean product action.
- **Callback:** a visual or line from the opening returns with a changed meaning at the payoff or CTA.
- **Status reversal:** the underestimated user/product becomes the competent one through a visible demonstration.

Avoid humor that depends on humiliating a customer, mocking disability or protected traits, unsafe imitation, fake emergencies, sexual coercion, or a serious harm claim. Keep the brand and purchaser likable.

## Retention rhythm

Use timing as a diagnostic, not a rigid template.

Only when the user requested a 15-second ad, a useful starting shape is:

- `0.0–1.5s` pattern break plus a legible question/problem;
- `1.5–4.0s` comic setup and product identification;
- `4.0–8.5s` escalation plus product action/mechanism;
- `8.5–12.0s` visible proof and punchline/callback;
- `12.0–15.0s` offer/CTA with enough reading time.

Create a meaningful visual, narrative, or audio change roughly every two to three seconds without sacrificing comprehension. Put the product on screen early unless a deliberate reveal improves clarity. The first frame should work muted; essential claims must not rely only on dialogue.

For 30 seconds, develop the relationship and a second escalation rather than adding filler. For a longer story, earn its measured length with motivated exchanges and a second turn while planning per-scene production handoffs. Preserve the conversation across that boundary; follow [model-routing.md](model-routing.md).

## Hook design

When hook variants are requested, develop alternative modules sharing the same body:

- **Visual contradiction:** a surprising but safe state visible in the first frame.
- **Specific confession/question:** language taken from real audience friction, not generic “You need this.”
- **Result-first:** show the supported outcome, then reveal how it happened.

Each hook must hand off cleanly to the same body at a defined frame, pose, line, or sound cue. Do not change the promise and visual style simultaneously when the variant is intended to isolate hook performance.

## Variant manifest

Plan modular production before generation:

| Variant | Hook | Body | Payoff | CTA | Thumbnail | Changed variable |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | H-A | B-1 | P-A | CTA-A | T-A | Baseline |
| V2 | H-B | B-1 | P-A | CTA-A | T-A | Hook only |
| V3 | H-C | B-1 | P-A | CTA-A | T-A | Hook only |

Default production is one selected master. Produce additional hook variants or thumbnails only within the requested/approved scope; four concept drafts do not authorize four rendered videos. Extra payoff or CTA variants should reuse the approved body and become separate tests, not be multiplied into every possible combination automatically.

## Script and on-screen text

Write the whole scene before dividing it into shots. Record speaker, listener, intent, preceding cue, and reaction for each exchange. Preserve questions and answers, misunderstandings and corrections, interruption timing, and pauses; never flatten the scene into sales slogans or a chain of comeback lines. Write speakable lines and measure them with actual audition/playback before locking timing. Prefer concrete verbs and short phrases. Put exact prices, promo codes, disclaimers, logos, labels, and CTA typography on editable editor tracks. Do not ask an image/video generator to render mission-critical small text.

Use a clear CTA that matches the destination: shop, view details, claim an actually available offer, choose a size, or learn more. Avoid unsupported urgency, fake countdowns, or “everyone is buying” claims. Preserve necessary disclosures on screen long enough to read.

## Thumbnail candidates

Use a clean product silhouette, a readable reaction or consequence, and one short truthful phrase. Preserve safe areas for the intended platform. Do not use a fabricated result, fake UI notification, misleading play button, fake news style, or a product state absent from the video/landing page.
